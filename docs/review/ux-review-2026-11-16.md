# Automation Portal: UI, UX and feature review (2026-11-16)

Read-only design review. Nothing was built or launched. The UI was judged from the six Store screenshots
(`docs/store/screenshots/01` to `06`) and from XAML and code. It builds on `suite-review-2026-11-11.md`
and does not repeat it; section 6 lists which of its findings are still open.

Path shorthand: **App** = `src/AutomationPortal.App`, **Ed** = `src/AutomationPortal.Editor`,
**Ed builder partial** = the second partial class file of `ShortcutEditorWindow` (the one that holds the
plain-language builder and recording). Effort: **S** up to 2 days, **M** up to 2 weeks, **L** more.

## 1. Summary verdict

1. The base is strong: sentence-style steps, friendly empty states, plain error text, Recently deleted,
   version history, file-change undo and a consent prompt for outside callers. The look is calm and consistent.
2. It is not yet beginner-safe. One click on a tile runs it, including shortcuts that move files, and
   Edit sits behind a hover-only "…" or a right-click.
3. The welcome is one dense dialog with three jobs. Nobody sees a shortcut actually do something in it.
4. Words collide: "shortcut" and "keyboard shortcut", "automation" and "trigger", "action" and "step".
5. Scheduling takes four screens and about ten clicks for "every day at 8". Hotkey and triggers hide in Details.
6. The editor's centre is good. Its edges lead with the hardest things: "Scripting" is the first category,
   "Run step by step" is a permanent header button, variables appear only on "{" or right-click.
7. The power layer exists (CLI `ap`, links, pipe, export, versions) but has no surface in the app at all.
8. Safety is well engineered and under-presented: Undo is offered in two places, never after a normal run,
   and preview exists only as a per-step tick box under "Show More".
9. Accessibility has gaps: no high-contrast handling, white text on yellow and green tiles, 11 to 12 px text,
   no access keys, no localisation layer.
10. Verdict: a solid B. Every P1 below is S or M. Batches 1 to 4 in section 9 reach the bar for beginners.

## 2. Clicks for the common jobs

| Job | Today | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Run a shortcut | 1 click, a hotkey, or Quick Run | same |
| Edit a shortcut | 2, both hidden (right-click, or hover "…") | 1 visible |
| Add from the Gallery | 3 (Gallery, card, Add Shortcut) plus questions | 2 (Add on the card) |
| Give it a hotkey | 3 plus the keys (right-click, Keyboard Shortcut…, Save) | 2 from the "When" card |
| Run it every day at 8 | 10 to 11 across 4 screens | 4 to 5 on 2 screens |
| See why it failed | 1 ("See details" on the red bar) | same |
| Undo its file changes | 4 (Run History, run, Undo file changes, Undo) | 2 (Undo on the result bar, confirm) |
| Restore a deleted shortcut | 2 to 3 | 1 (Undo bar after delete) |
| Import a shortcut file | not possible in the app once the Gallery has items | 2 |
| Copy a link or command for a shortcut | not possible in the app | 2 |

## 7. Fitting the planned features

### 7.1 Templates gallery

**Where it lives.** At the top of the Gallery as a "Start here" row of five wide cards, above "Get Started".
The same five appear as chips in the New Shortcut menu ("From a template"), on welcome page 1, and in the
empty editor. A template differs from a ready-made shortcut: it asks two or three questions and can add
its own automation. The parts exist already: import questions (`Ed/DetailsSheet.cs:154`,
`GalleryPage.xaml.cs:189-192`) and "add with automation" (`GalleryPage.xaml.cs:141`).

**How a card looks.** Twice the width of a Gallery card. Icon, name, one sentence, and three small step
icons that tell the story (folder, arrow, sorted folders). Corner badge: "Changes files" or "Needs Excel".

**The set-up sheet.** One screen, a sentence with blanks, no wizard:

> Every **day ▾** at **18:00**, move files in **Downloads** that are older than **7 days** into folders by **type ▾**.
>
> Preview: 23 files would move. *Show list*
>
> [ Add and turn on ]   [ Add, don't schedule yet ]

| Template | Builds on | Safe defaults |
|---|---|---|
| Tidy Downloads | `gallery/organize-downloads`, `tidy-desktop` | Preview count before adding; first run shows the Undo bar; never touches files in use or newer than a day. |
| Convert a batch of files | `resize-images`, `images-to-pdf`, `combine-pdfs` | Always writes copies next to the originals or into a folder the user picks; names never collide ("name (2)"). |
| Set up my workspace | `open-work-apps`, `start-my-workday`, `focus-mode` | App picker with tick boxes for the apps open right now ("Use what's open now"). No file changes. |
| Back up a folder on a schedule | new | Copy only; never deletes at the destination; skips with a note when the drive is missing; "If missed, run when the PC wakes" on. |
| Fill a form from a spreadsheet | new, uses 7.2 | Runs row 1 only, step by step, the first time; then "Run the other 41 rows". Stops on the first row that does not match. |

### 7.2 Record and repeat (UI Automation based)

**Where it lives.** A third equal choice in the New Shortcut menu ("Record what you do"), and "Record"
beside "Add step" in the editor, which inserts at the selection. Not in the tray or the Quick Panel until
it has proved reliable.

**How it flows.**

1. **Before recording.** A small sheet: "Which app will you show?" with the open windows as tiles, then a
   3-2-1 countdown. Choosing the app sets the scope and keeps other apps out of the recording.
2. **While recording.** The existing bar (`App/Shell/RecordingBarWindow.cs`) gains a live list of the last
   three captured steps as sentences: "Click **Save** in Notepad", "Type 'Hello' into **File name**". Names
   come from the UI Automation element, so the user sees that the app understood.
3. **After Stop.** The steps arrive in the editor inside one block, "Recorded in Notepad". Each step shows a
   target chip (element name and a small picture of it). A step that could only be captured by position
   gets a caution badge, "By position: may break if the window moves", with **Pick the control again**.
4. **Make it repeat.** Select a typed value and choose "Different each time": **Ask me**, **From the
   clipboard**, or **From a spreadsheet column**. This is the bridge to the form-fill template.
5. **First replay.** Runs step by step automatically, with the target highlighted before each click.

**Safety.** Password fields are never recorded (shown as "not recorded"). Recording pauses on a Windows
security prompt. The bar always shows that recording is on, with Stop and its hotkey.

## 10. Delight

Added 2026-11-17 at the owner's request: make the app delightful to use, like a good macOS or iOS app.
Batches 1 to 8 are built, so this section starts from the app as it is now (code read, nothing launched).
Delight here is not decoration. It is the feeling that the app is fast, on your side and finished.
New items are numbered **D-1** to **D-22**; section 10.8 puts them in build order as Batch 9.

### 10.1 Principles

1. **Instant.** Every click, key and hover is answered in the same frame. Work that takes longer shows
   that it started, then what it is doing.
2. **Forgiving.** Anything the person does can be taken back from where they did it, with one click or
   Ctrl+Z. The result bar, Recently deleted, version history and the editor's undo already do this; no new
   feature may be the exception.
3. **Quiet confidence.** The app says what it did in one sentence with real numbers
   (`"Tidy Downloads" moved 14 files.`) and then stops talking. No praise, no exclamation marks.
4. **Considered defaults.** The first choice offered is the safe, likely one. Sound off, preview on for
   anything that changes files, the window where it was left.
5. **Motion with a job.** Movement shows where something came from, where it went or that it worked.
   If it does none of these, it is removed.
6. **Craft in small things.** Focus rings, spacing, wording and key hints are the same everywhere.
7. **Never a surprise with data.** No effect, shortcut key or drag may change, move or delete a file
   without the preview, confirm and Undo rules that a normal run follows.

### 10.2 Motion

| Moment | Now | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| Tile hover | Grows to 1.025 in 120 ms, back in 180 ms, white veil to 14% | Keep |
| Tile press | Black veil at 16%, instant, nothing moves | Shrink to 0.97 in 80 ms while held, back to 1 in 160 ms on release. Same for the Run and Edit buttons |
| Run starts | Spinner shows at once, so a run of 30 ms flashes it | Spinner only if the run is still going after 150 ms, faded in over 100 ms |
| Run worked | Tick pops from 0.3 to 1 in 280 ms with a small overshoot, stays 1.8 s, then vanishes | Keep the pop: it is the app's one bouncy motion. Fade out in 200 ms |
| Run failed | The same bouncy pop as success, gone after 3 s | No bounce: fade in 120 ms. Stays for as long as its problem bar is showing |
| Result bar (library) | Appears and vanishes at once, the tiles below jump | In: fade and slide down 8 px in 180 ms, the tiles move with it. Out: 120 ms. After Undo the text cross-fades to "Undone" in 120 ms |
| Dialogs and sheets (the common dialog, template sheet, automation flow, welcome) | Fade in 140 ms, close at once, pages swap at once | Open: fade and grow from 0.97 to 1 in 160 ms. Close: fade 100 ms. Pages: slide 24 px and fade in 180 ms, from the right for Next, from the left for Back |
| Quick Run, Quick Panel | Slide 10 px in 170 to 180 ms | Keep. The Ctrl+K palette has no motion: give it the same. All three close at once |
| Add a step (editor) | The row appears and the list scrolls to it | The row opens from zero height and fades in over 160 ms. Focus lands in its first empty field |
| Delete a step | The row vanishes | The row closes in 120 ms |
| Reorder steps | A drop line, rows jump on drop | The dragged row lifts (shadow, slightly larger). Neighbours slide apart in 140 ms. The drop settles in 160 ms. Alt+Up and Alt+Down use the same slide |
| Results drawer (editor) | Opens in 180 ms | Keep |
| Automation switched on or off | Only the switch moves | The "next run" line fades in or out in 150 ms, so the switch visibly did something |
| Delete or restore a tile | The grid redraws | The tile fades and shrinks in 140 ms, the others close the gap in 180 ms. Undo plays it backwards |

Rules for all of it:

- **One place.** Every transition goes through `Motion.cs`, so with Windows "Animation effects" off it
  jumps to its end state and nothing is lost. Add named roles instead of more numbers: Press 80, Fast 120,
  Enter 160 to 180, Leave 100 to 120, Emphasis 280. Nothing is longer than 300 ms.
- **Live setting.** Durations in XAML are read once when a control is built, so switching the Windows
  setting while the app runs only reaches new controls. Make it apply without a restart.
- **Easing.** Things arriving slow down (cubic ease-out). Things leaving speed up and take about a third
  less time. Overshoot is used for the success tick only.
- **Never in the way.** Every animation can be interrupted. A click or key during one acts on the end
  state. Surfaces opened by a hotkey (Quick Run, Quick Panel, palette) never animate on close.
- **Cheap.** Animate position, size-by-transform and opacity only. Real layout changes are limited to one
  row or one bar at a time.
- **High contrast.** `HighContrast.cs` turns both the success and the failure colour into the system
  highlight colour, so the tick and the cross must stay different shapes with different names. Hover and
  press show a system-colour border in place of the white and black veils. Shadows are off. No state is
  shown by colour or opacity alone.

### 10.3 Feedback and moments

- **The first real run (D-9).** The welcome already runs "Say hello". The first time a shortcut the person
  added or made runs and succeeds, the result bar adds one line, once:
  `"Tidy Downloads" moved 14 files. That was your first shortcut.` with Undo and **Give it a hotkey**.
  The tick is the celebration. No confetti.
- **A run that changed no files stays quiet:** the tick on the tile, no bar. This is how it works now. Keep it.
- **The first time an automation runs by itself (D-12).** One notification, once per automation:
  `"Tidy Downloads" just ran by itself for the first time. It moved 14 files.` with **See what it did**
  and Undo. After that the normal notification settings apply, and the row on the Automations page carries
  the history: "Ran 12 times. Last: today 08:00."
- **Stats, only honest ones (D-13).** Run history stores how long the PC took, which is not the time a
  person saved. So there is no "saved you 2 hours" by default. Show counts that are simply true, at the top
  of Run history: "This month: 86 runs, 41 by themselves, 310 files handled." Count only as far back as
  the records go, and say so ("since 3 November") when older records have been trimmed.
- **Time saved, by the person's own estimate (D-20, later).** One optional field in Details: "By hand this
  takes me about … minutes". Only shortcuts that have it are counted, and the line always reads
  "About 3 hours saved this month, by your estimates". No streaks, badges or levels: this is a tool.
- **Sound (D-16).** Off by default. One switch in Settings next to "Tell me when a shortcut started by
  hotkey finishes": "Play a sound when a shortcut I started finishes". Two soft sounds under 400 ms (done,
  problem). Silent when the PC is muted, in Do not disturb or while a full-screen app is in front. Runs
  that start by themselves never play the done sound.
- **Long runs (D-11).** Today the tile only shows a spinner. After 2 seconds the tile shows the current
  step under its name ("Step 3 of 7: Resize pictures") and, in a loop over a known list, a real count
  ("12 of 40 files"). No invented percentages. The taskbar button shows progress when the count is real
  and "busy" when it is not. Starting a running shortcut again already stops it; make that visible: the
  Run button reads Stop while it runs.
- **The tray (D-22).** The icon's tooltip says what is running, or what comes next
  ("Next: Tidy Downloads at 08:00").

### 10.4 Words

The voice is already right: "didn't finish", "put back", "for good", "Let's make something happen",
"Nothing was overwritten". Write it down as a one-page voice sheet in `docs` (D-7) with these rules:

1. Say what happened in the person's words, with the shortcut's name in quotes and a real number.
2. Say what is safe: "Nothing was overwritten." "Your files were not changed."
3. Offer one next step as a button with a verb on it: "Choose another folder", "Try again", "Open Settings".
4. Never: "Oops", "Successfully", "Error:", "Invalid", codes before words, exclamation marks.

Fixes found while reading:

- Raw system text reaches people in at least two places: the "Couldn't undo" bar in the library and
  "Automation Portal couldn't make the sample files: …" in Test now. Lead with a plain sentence and move
  the system text behind "See details".
- Problem lines on automation rows ("The last run didn't finish: …", "Skipped last time: …", "This
  automation can't run…") each get the button that fixes them, as "Paused" already has (D-15).
- Empty states keep three parts: what this is, why it is empty, one button. Check Run history, Snippets
  and Automations against the library's. Add two: the palette with no match
  (`Nothing called "inv". Press Enter to make a new shortcut with that name.`) and Quick Run with an empty
  box showing the last five runs (they are already stored).
- Buttons are verbs. A destructive button names what it destroys ("Delete shortcut", never "OK").
- Tooltips carry the key: "Run (Enter)", "Edit (Ctrl+E)", "Rename (F2)".

### 10.5 Speed and perceived speed

Budgets to measure first, on a mid-range laptop, and to log in debug builds (D-6):

| Thing | Budget |
|---|---|
| Tray click or hotkey to the window on screen (app already running) | 100 ms |
| Quick Run or palette open and ready for typing | 80 ms |
| Keystroke to updated results, 1,000 shortcuts, no delay timer | 30 ms |
| Tile click to visible press | 1 frame |
| Editor open for a 50-step shortcut | 300 ms |
| Cold start to tray icon and working hotkeys | 1.5 s |
| Cold start to the library drawn | 2.5 s |

- **Known cost: the palette.** Every Ctrl+K rebuilds the whole list and reads the Gallery from disk.
  Build it once and refresh it when the library changes.
- **Possible cost: tile shadows.** Every tile has a live drop shadow and scales on hover. Test scrolling
  with 300 tiles. If it stutters, cache each tile as a bitmap or draw the shadow once (D-21).
- **Good now:** closing the main window hides it, so it comes back at once, and the result sentence is
  worked out off the UI thread.
- **Search as you type.** Filter in place on every key, bold the matched letters, keep the first result
  selected so Enter runs it.
- **Optimistic UI, where safe.** Pin, rename, reorder, move to a folder, colour and icon, automation on or
  off, delete to Recently deleted: show the change at once, save behind it, and put it back with a plain
  line if saving fails.
- **Never optimistic about files.** The tick appears only when the run has really finished. The result bar
  reports only what the file-change record holds. A preview never looks like a finished run. Undo reports
  item by item, as it does now.

### 10.6 Small craft details

- **The window remembers (D-1).** The main window always opens at 1180 by 760 in the centre of the screen.
  Remember size, position, maximised and the last page, and the editor's size too, in `app-ui.json`.
  If the saved place is off-screen (a monitor was unplugged), fall back to the centre.
- **Focus rings (D-8).** Six button and list styles switch off the standard focus mark. The tile draws its
  own ring; check that the other five do. One ring everywhere: 2 px, 1 px gap, visible on every tile colour.
- **Hover and press** exist on every clickable thing, including list rows, sidebar folders and Gallery cards.
- **Materials.** Rule: windows that stay use Mica, surfaces that pop up and go use Acrylic. Quick Run and
  Quick Panel follow it. The palette pops up but uses Mica: move it to Acrylic (D-4).
- **Type.** Titles of 20 px and above use the display font that is already defined. Secondary text is 13 px
  where space allows and never below 12.
- **Spacing.** One scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24. Tile margins (6) and bar and card padding (14 by 10, 12 by 10)
  move onto it.
- **Icons.** Outline icons at 20 or 24 everywhere. Filled is used only for "selected" or "on".
- **Keyboard.** Enter, Ctrl+E, F2, Delete, Ctrl+D and Ctrl+Z work on tiles today. Add Space on a selected
  tile for "Show what it would do", like Quick Look (D-18). Every row type (automation, history, snippet,
  Gallery card) gets the same keys where they make sense.
- **Right-click.** Tiles have a menu on right-click, the Menu key and Shift+F10. Every row and card with a
  "…" button gets the same menu the same three ways, and so do sidebar folders.
- **Drag and drop.** Dragging a tile shows the lifted tile, not a bare cursor. Dropping files on a tile that
  takes files runs it with them (D-17), through the same preview and Undo as any run.

### 10.7 What not to do

- No confetti, sound or bounce on anything that deletes, moves or replaces files. Those moments are calm:
  one sentence and Undo.
- No celebration for a failed or half-finished run, and no bouncy motion on failures.
- No nagging: no "rate us" prompts, streak reminders, tips that come back after being closed, or
  "you haven't run this in a while".
- No upsell or sibling-app adverts in front of a task. Connected apps stay in Settings.
- No made-up numbers: no time saved without the person's estimate, no progress bar without a real count.
- No motion that slows experts: nothing over 300 ms, nothing that blocks a key press, no animation on
  closing hotkey surfaces, no animated page changes when moving with Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6.
- No sound by default, and never for a run nobody started by hand.
- No optimistic "done" for a run, and no auto-hiding bar while the pointer or keyboard focus is in it.
- No second design language: no custom spinners, toasts or dialog styles next to the ones that exist.
